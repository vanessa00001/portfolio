Requirements Decision Record Template

A one-page, fillable record for capturing the decisions behind your requirements: the decision, its rationale, owner, assumptions, and revisit trigger.

VS Vanessa Salonoy Jul 22, 2026 2 min read Foundational Business Analysis & Requirements #requirements #decision records #templates #business analysis #governance

Requirements lose the reasoning behind them, so decisions get re-argued and change turns into disputes.

DOCX

What this is

A one-page record for capturing the decision behind a significant requirement. It makes the reasoning explicit, assigns an owner, and sets a trigger for when the decision should be revisited. It is the working companion to the Insight Requirements Are Decisions, Not Documents, which explains why this matters. This page gives you the artifact to use.

When to use it

Use a Requirements Decision Record for the decisions that carry weight: choices that are contested, expensive, hard to reverse, or regulated. Do not attach one to every requirement. Applied to everything, it becomes the heavy document it is meant to replace. The skill is choosing the vital few.

The template

Copy this into your requirements tool, wiki, or document. Keep one record per significant decision.

FieldYour entry
DecisionThe choice being made, in one sentence. Not the requirement text, the decision inside it.
RationaleWhy this option was chosen, in enough detail that someone new understands it.
OwnerThe person accountable for the decision. A named role at minimum, never a committee.
AssumptionsWhat must stay true for the decision to hold. Be specific; these are your early warnings.
Revisit whenThe event or date that reopens the decision. This is what keeps the record alive.

How to fill it in

  • Decision. State the actual choice, not the requirement. “Configure the standard tax engine” is a decision; “the system shall calculate tax” is not.
  • Rationale. Capture the business reason a signature would otherwise discard.
  • Owner. Name someone accountable. A decision without an owner is a wish.
  • Assumptions. List the conditions the decision depends on. When one breaks, you want to notice.
  • Revisit when. Give it a trigger. This is what turns a static note into a living decision.

Worked example

A finance transformation deciding how to handle a country-specific tax rule:

FieldEntry
DecisionConfigure the platform’s standard tax engine rather than build custom logic.
RationalePreserve upgradeability and lower total cost of ownership; the standard engine covers the current rules.
OwnerFinance transformation lead (business); solution architect accountable for feasibility.
AssumptionsThe jurisdiction’s rules stay within the standard engine; no bespoke rounding rule is mandated.
Revisit whenA jurisdiction needs a rule the engine cannot express, or a new entity is onboarded.

When a new entity later needs a bespoke rounding rule, the “revisit when” trigger has fired. The team reopens the record, sees why “configure” was chosen and what would change it, and makes the new call deliberately, with a clean trail. The dispute never happens.

Where this fits

Pair this template with the Insight it comes from, Requirements Are Decisions, Not Documents, for the full reasoning. As your practice matures, the same discipline extends into a requirements sign-off readiness checklist and a full elicitation playbook.
