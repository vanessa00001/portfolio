Enterprise Data Governance Playbook

A complete methodology for governing data quality, ownership, lifecycle, master data, and compliance, and for building AI-ready data foundations.

VS Vanessa Salonoy Jul 22, 2026 13 min read Intermediate Governance, Risk & Assurance #data governance #data quality #master data #compliance #AI readiness

By the end you will have: A governed data estate - critical entities owned and stewarded, quality measured against rules that matter, lifecycle and compliance managed by design, and foundations AI can be trusted on - run as standing capability, not a project.

  • Time to execute4 to 8 weeks to establish the operating model; governs the estate for its life
  • RolesData Owner (business), Data Steward, Enterprise Architect, Compliance Lead
  • PrerequisitesAn inventory of critical data entities (even a rough one), Named executive sponsorship for data accountability, The delivery context creating demand (programs, integrations, AI initiatives)

Executive overview

Every enterprise already governs its data; most just do it accidentally, through whoever last touched the spreadsheet, whichever system shouted loudest, and whatever the auditors found. The symptoms are familiar: two reports that disagree and a meeting to negotiate which is true, migrations that stall on quality nobody measured, compliance exercises rebuilt from scratch each year, and now AI initiatives confidently automating on data no one would sign their name to.

Data governance done deliberately is none of the bureaucracy its reputation suggests. It is a small set of owned decisions, who owns each critical entity, what quality means for it, where truth lives, how long data exists and on what terms, applied as standing capability rather than a project. This playbook establishes that capability. It extends the delivery sequence, the Discovery, Requirements Management, Solution Design, and Integration Strategy playbooks, into the asset those programs all touch and none of them owns: the data itself.

When to use this playbook

Use it when transformation raises the stakes: an ERP or CRM program about to migrate what it finds, an integration strategy formalizing where truth lives, a compliance obligation tired of annual heroics, or an AI initiative whose appetite for data has outrun anyone’s accountability for it. Use it before the crisis where possible and during one where necessary; establish the operating model once, then govern by exception. Scale by criticality: govern the twenty entities that run the business deliberately, and let the long tail be governed by convention.

Principles

  1. Data rulings are decisions. System of record, quality thresholds, retention terms, and access grants follow the RDR Model discipline: owned, reasoned, revisitable.
  2. Ownership is business ownership. Data is owned where its meaning lives, by named business roles; technology stewards the plumbing, never the truth.
  3. Quality is fitness, not perfection. Data is good when it is fit for the decisions and processes that consume it, measured against rules those consumers set.
  4. Truth is singular and declared. Every critical entity has one system of record, decided once, recorded, and enforced at the boundaries.
  5. Lifecycle by design. Creation, use, retention, and disposal are decided when data is born, not discovered when a regulator asks.
  6. Governed data enables; ungoverned data taxes. The measure of the capability is faster programs, confident automation, and calm audits, not the thickness of its policy binder.

From the delivery playbooks to data governance

The delivery sequence generates data decisions constantly; governance is where they live and stay honest. Discovery’s data layer (where truth lives, where reconciliation effort is spent) becomes the entity inventory’s first draft. Solution design’s data rulings (system of record, ownership, movement) are governance decisions made in delivery context, and they land in the governance record rather than dying with the program. Integration’s truth rulings and contracts define what crosses boundaries and on whose authority. The handoff rule is the same as the rest of the sequence: made mechanical. Every program-level data decision is recorded once, in the shared governance record, and inherited by the next program instead of re-litigated by it.

Ownership and stewardship

Two roles, deliberately distinct. The data owner is the business role accountable for an entity: its meaning, its quality rules, its access terms, and the rulings when consumers disagree. Ownership is per entity and named; a committee that owns everything owns nothing. The data steward does the standing work on the owner’s behalf: monitoring quality, triaging issues, maintaining metadata, and shepherding changes. Stewardship is where governance most often dies of aspiration, so define it as observable behaviors with evidence, the Adoption Ledger discipline applied to a standing role: which checks run, which issues get triaged within what window, which metadata stays current. A stewardship model that cannot be evidenced is an org chart decoration.

The full ownership model distinguishes five roles, and the line that matters runs between strategic accountability and operational responsibility:

RoleAccountabilityIn practice
Executive sponsorStrategic: that critical data is governed at all, and fundedRules on cross-entity disputes; owns the capability’s mandate
Data ownerStrategic: an entity’s meaning, quality rules, and access termsThe business role who decides; one per entity, named
Data stewardOperational: the standing work of quality, triage, and metadataActs on the owner’s behalf, with evidenced behaviors
Data custodianOperational: the technical safekeeping of the dataRuns the platforms, pipelines, controls, and backups to the rules
Data consumerOperational: fit use, and the duty to report unfitnessUses data within its terms; feeds issues back to stewards

Sponsors and owners are accountable for what the data means and may be used for; stewards, custodians, and consumers are responsible for what happens to it day to day. Collapse the two and governance fails in one of two ways: executives ruling on field formats, or engineers ruling on meaning.

Data quality management

Quality starts with the consumers: for each critical entity, the processes and decisions that consume it define fitness, and fitness becomes a small set of measurable rules drawn from six dimensions:

DimensionThe question it answersMeasured by
AccuracyDoes the value reflect reality?Verification against the authoritative source or the world
CompletenessIs everything that should be present, present?Required-field and required-record coverage
ConsistencyDoes the same fact agree with itself across systems?Cross-system comparison on shared keys
TimelinessIs the data current enough for its use?Age against the consuming process’s tolerance
ValidityDoes the value conform to its rules and formats?Rule and domain checks at entry and at rest
UniquenessDoes each real-world thing exist exactly once?Duplicate detection on the entity’s identity

Each dimension is measured independently, because they fail independently: a record can be complete and stale, valid and wrong, unique and inconsistent with its twin in the next system. Improving one dimension does not automatically improve the others, deduplication does nothing for accuracy, and a timely feed can deliver invalid values faster, so an entity’s rules name which dimensions its consumers actually depend on, and the measurement reports them separately. Rules are owned by the data owner, measured where the data lives, and reported as claims with evidence per the Evidence-Backed Status discipline: the rule, the current measure, the trend, and the decision requested of the owner. Remediate at source where the source is reachable, at the boundary where it is not, and record the choice; a downstream cleansing routine nobody remembers is an incident with a delay on it.

Master data and truth

Master data, the entities many systems share (customers, products, suppliers, employees, chart of accounts), is where the singular-truth principle earns its keep. Per entity: one declared system of record; declared survivorship rules where records merge; and boundaries that enforce the ruling, which is exactly the contract discipline the Integration Strategy Playbook establishes. Whether the architecture centralizes mastering in a dedicated platform or federates it across systems is a platform decision, made against the Constraint Horizon questions, not against a demo. What governance insists on either way: the ruling exists, is recorded, and is enforced somewhere real.

Metadata management

Metadata is the estate’s self-knowledge: what each entity means, where it lives, how it flows, who owns it, and what rules bind it. Keep the ambition honest, a maintained glossary of critical entities, lineage for the flows that matter (regulatory reporting, financial truth, AI training and inference paths), and currency as a stewardship behavior, beats an enterprise catalog bought in optimism and abandoned in year two. The test is usability: a new analyst, architect, or auditor can answer “what is this field, where does it come from, and can I trust it” without a meeting.

Lifecycle and compliance

Data is governed across eight lifecycle stages, each with its responsibilities and expected controls:

StageGovernance responsibilityExpected controls
CreateOwner rules on purpose and classification at birthPurpose recorded; sensitivity classified; owner assigned
CaptureStewards enforce quality at the point of entryValidity and completeness checks; source recorded for lineage
ValidateQuality rules applied before data is trustedDimension checks against the entity’s rules; failures routed to stewards
StoreCustodians keep it safe on the owner’s termsAccess per least privilege; protection matching classification; backups tested
ShareBoundaries enforce truth and contractsSystem-of-record ruling honored; contracts versioned; recipients within purpose
UseConsumers stay within purpose; unfitness reportedPurpose limitation; usage auditable for the sensitive tier
ArchiveRetention terms applied, retrieval preservedRetention per the recorded term; access narrowed; retrieval tested
RetireDisposal actually deletes, and can prove itCertified deletion across copies; disposal evidenced for audit

Lifecycle terms are decided at creation: why the data exists (purpose), how long it lives (retention), who may see it (access, least-privilege by default), and how it dies (disposal that actually deletes). Compliance then stops being a project: obligations (privacy regimes, sector rules, contractual terms) are mapped to entities, the entities’ lifecycle terms implement the obligations, and evidence accumulates as a byproduct. Access and retention rulings on sensitive entities are the vital few in the Proportionate Assurance sense: independently verified, not self-attested. The goal, as everywhere in this library, is the calm audit: the trail exists because the work made it.

AI-ready data foundations

AI raises the stakes on every section above, because models consume data at scale and act on it with confidence. AI-ready means, concretely: training and inference data whose lineage is known (what fed this model, from where, as of when); quality measured on the paths AI consumes, with thresholds that gate automation; access and purpose rules that survive the tempting shortcut of “just point it at everything”; and staleness treated as a failure mode, wired to the Escalation Seam so automation stands down when its data degrades rather than acting on yesterday’s truth. The Toil-Judgment Line’s placement discipline applies to governance itself: automate the measuring and monitoring toil; keep the meaning rulings, quality thresholds, and access grants with accountable humans. An organization that cannot say what fed its model has not deployed AI; it has deployed a liability with a dashboard.

Responsibilities, decision rights, and escalation, published and short:

ComplianceObligation mapping, verification of the vital fewWhat requires independent verificationExecutive sponsor

Escalation is the short path from a steward’s finding to an owner’s ruling; a quality issue that waits for a quarterly council was escalated to a calendar, not a decision-maker. Governance ceremonies are proportionate: a standing data council for cross-entity rulings and scope, and everything else by exception.

Metrics

Measure the capability by what it enables. Leading: critical entities with named owners (should be all, and the trend is the truth), quality rules measured on schedule, stewardship behaviors evidenced, data decisions recorded versus re-litigated. Lagging: report disputes (“which number is true” meetings, trending down), migration and integration delays attributed to data, audit findings and their recurrence, and AI incidents traced to data staleness or lineage gaps. Report both sets as claims with evidence, and let time-to-trust, how quickly a new program or model can rely on an entity, become the number that buys the budget.

Common mistakes

  • Governance as a project: a framework launched, a binder produced, a capability never operated.
  • Technology ownership: stewards ruling on meaning because the business never showed up.
  • Perfection quality: measuring everything against everything instead of fitness against use.
  • The catalog gambit: buying metadata software instead of maintaining metadata behaviors.
  • Truth by negotiation: system of record decided per meeting, per report, forever.
  • Compliance by reconstruction: the annual scramble instead of lifecycle by design.
  • AI on ungoverned data: automating confidently on inputs nobody owns.
  • Governing the long tail with the same rigor as the critical twenty, and drowning.

Data governance maturity model

LevelNameCharacteristics
Level 1ReactiveTruth by negotiation; quality discovered in incidents; compliance by heroics
Level 2DefinedCritical entities inventoried; owners named; rules written for the worst pain
Level 3ManagedQuality measured against recorded rules; lifecycle terms applied; issues routed to owners
Level 4GovernedStewardship evidenced; proportionate verification on the vital few; boundaries enforce truth; audits calm
Level 5Data-Driven EnterpriseData governed as a strategic asset: programs inherit rulings, AI gates on quality and lineage, and decisions across the enterprise run on data everyone can trust

Organizations mature from isolated practices to enterprise governance in that order: from reacting to incidents (Level 1), to defining ownership and rules (Level 2), to operating measurement and lifecycle (Level 3), to governing with evidence and proportionate verification (Level 4), until governed data stops being a program and becomes the ground the enterprise decides on (Level 5). One habit per program, made standard.

Deliverables

  1. The critical entity inventory with named owners and stewards.
  2. The governance record: quality rules, truth rulings, lifecycle and access terms, all in decision form.
  3. The quality measurement pack, reported as evidence per entity.
  4. The obligation-to-entity compliance map with its verification evidence.
  5. The AI data readiness record: lineage, gates, and stand-down conditions for automated consumers.
  6. The operating model: roles, decision rights, escalation, and ceremony cadence.

Learning path

This playbook governs the asset the delivery sequence keeps touching. Behind it, in order: the Enterprise Discovery Playbook where the data layer is first evidenced, the Enterprise Requirements Management Playbook where data requirements join the baseline, the Enterprise Solution Design Playbook where the rulings are designed, and the Enterprise Integration Strategy Playbook where they are enforced at boundaries. The conceptual spine remains Requirements Are Decisions, Not Documents with its template, and around it: Assurance Is What Lets Leaders Say Yes for the verification tiers, Adoption Is the Real Go-Live for evidenced stewardship, Automate the Toil, Not the Judgment for AI gating and the governance of automation itself, and The Status Report That Tells the Truth for how data quality reaches its owners.

How this playbook uses the framework library

The pattern, applied to the estate’s most-shared asset: the RDR Model turns every data ruling into an owned, revisitable decision. Evidence-Backed Status carries quality reporting. The Adoption Ledger makes stewardship observable. Proportionate Assurance scales verification to consequence. The Constraint Horizon governs mastering and platform choices. The Toil-Judgment Line and Escalation Seam gate what AI may consume and when it must stand down. One system, one language, now spanning discovery, requirements, design, integration, and the data underneath all of it.
