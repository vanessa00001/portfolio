Enterprise Project Delivery Playbook

A complete methodology for planning, governing, delivering, and improving enterprise transformation across Agile, Waterfall, and hybrid delivery models.

VS Vanessa Salonoy Jul 22, 2026 10 min read Intermediate Project & Delivery Management #project delivery #program management #delivery controls #governance #transformation

By the end you will have: A governed delivery capability - honest baselines, evidence-backed controls, proportionate assurance, managed change, rehearsed cutover, and adoption tracked past go-live - that holds across Agile, Waterfall, and hybrid models.

  • Time to execute2 to 3 weeks to stand up on a program; operates for the life of the delivery
  • RolesProgram or Project Manager, Delivery Lead, PMO Lead, Executive Sponsor
  • PrerequisitesA mandate and scope worth delivering (discovery-grade evidence preferred), A named sponsor who will consume honest status, Agreement that controls exist to enable decisions, not to decorate them

Executive overview

Enterprise delivery does not usually fail loudly. It fails politely: baselines set in optimism, status softened one notch per week, risks rephrased as watch items, change resisted until it arrives as crisis, and a go-live celebrated while the transformation quietly dissolves behind it. Every one of those failures is a controls failure before it is a delivery failure, and the flagship Insight for this capability named the root: delivery is run on claims, and claims that do not owe evidence become theater.

This playbook is the delivery methodology built on the opposite premise. Planning produces honest baselines, controls produce decision-ready evidence, governance scales with consequence, change is governed rather than fought, cutover is rehearsed rather than hoped, and delivery does not end at go-live because the business event is adoption, not deployment. It wraps the whole sequence, the Discovery, Requirements Management, Solution Design, Integration Strategy, Data Governance, and AI & Automation Strategy playbooks, into one delivered outcome, and it is deliberately model-neutral: the controls are constant, and Agile, Waterfall, and hybrid are tempos they run at.

When to use this playbook

Use it on any initiative where the cost of drift exceeds the cost of discipline: ERP and CRM programs, integrations, replatforming, multi-vendor delivery, regulated change. Use it from day one where possible, and mid-flight where necessary; a program can adopt honest controls at any point that leadership can bear to hear what they say. Scale by consequence: a small delivery needs the baseline, the evidence-backed cadence, and the cutover discipline, and can carry the rest lightly.

Principles

  1. Baselines are decisions. Scope, schedule, and cost baselines follow the RDR Model: owned, reasoned, assumption-stated, revisitable, so re-baselining is a governed decision, not a defeat.
  2. Status is a claim that owes evidence. Every significant status line carries claim, evidence, trend, and decision requested, per the Evidence-Backed Status model.
  3. Assurance scales with consequence. Gates and reviews follow Proportionate Assurance: low-risk work moves at attestation speed, the vital few earn verification.
  4. Change is governed evolution. The baseline is defended by process, not by combat; assumptions firing are the system working.
  5. Go-live is a rehearsal away, and adoption is the finish line. Cutover is practiced, and the Adoption Ledger runs past demobilization.
  6. The model serves the delivery. Agile, Waterfall, or hybrid is chosen per workstream by the work’s properties, and the controls hold across all three.

From the sequence to delivery

Delivery inherits, and the inheritance is mechanical. Discovery supplies the evidence and the opening decision log. Requirements management supplies the baseline, its RDRs, and change impact discipline. Solution design supplies the gated design and its ADRs. Integration strategy supplies boundary health the cutover will depend on. Data governance supplies migration confidence, because go-live confidence is mostly data confidence. AI and automation strategy supplies flows with seams already designed. The delivery playbook’s job is not to redo any of it; it is to carry the whole inheritance across the line with the controls that keep it honest, and to hand back what delivery learns: fired assumptions, exercised seams, and the adoption evidence that says whether any of it mattered.

Planning and baselining

Plan against evidence, not ambition: discovery’s findings size the work, the requirements baseline scopes it, and the design’s constraint findings pace it. Then baseline as a set of recorded decisions, each with its owner, rationale, assumptions, and, critically, its re-baseline triggers, the conditions under which the plan is formally revisited rather than silently eroded. A schedule whose assumptions are stated can be defended and can be changed with dignity; a schedule that is just dates becomes theater the first week reality disagrees with it. Estimate honestly (ranges where uncertainty is real, confidence stated), keep float visible instead of hidden in padded tasks, and treat the benefits case as part of the baseline, because it is the number adoption will eventually be measured against.

Delivery models: Agile, Waterfall, and hybrid

Choose the model per workstream, by the work’s properties, not the organization’s fashion. Waterfall tempo fits work that is sequential, contract-bound, or physically staged: infrastructure, regulated milestones, vendor deliveries with fixed gates. Agile tempo fits work where learning changes the answer: configuration discovery, user-facing flows, anything where two-week evidence beats six-month prediction. Most enterprise programs are honestly hybrid, and the discipline is one spine, two tempos: a single traceable baseline and one set of controls, with the stable core run on milestones and the evolving edge run on backlog, exactly as the requirements and design playbooks established. What never varies by model: ownership, evidence-backed status, proportionate assurance, governed change, and the definition of done. A standup is not a control and a gate review is not a bureaucracy; both are containers, and what makes either real is the evidence inside it.

Delivery controls and cadence

The control set is small and constant: an evidence-backed status cadence, a risk register graded by consequence and reversibility, an action and decision log in RDR form, milestone or sprint evidence against the baseline, and a change flow. The cadence runs on the Evidence-Backed Status discipline end to end: workstreams report claims with evidence, trend, and the decision requested; the program rolls them up without softening; and the steering committee consumes decisions, not reassurance. Two rules keep the cadence honest. Trend is mandatory, because the weekly snapshot is where truth goes to hide. And the messenger is protected structurally: early bad news is rewarded in public at least once, early in the program, because the first punished messenger is the last honest report. Standing up this cadence quickly is its own craft; the delivery control cadence guide deepens it as it publishes.

Risk, assurance, and gates

Risk management and assurance are one system at two speeds. The register grades every risk by consequence and reversibility, mitigation is owned in the business or the workstream (never by the register itself), and the grading drives assurance per Proportionate Assurance: self-attestation for the routine, peer review for the material, independent verification for the vital few, go-live readiness, regulated milestones, irreversible data moves. Gates are decision points with the same three outcomes as the design playbook’s: approve, conditional with named owners and dates, or return, recorded every time. The program that runs this way arrives at its audits and its steering escalations already holding the evidence, because the controls made it as a byproduct.

Change control

Change is expected; drift is not. The flow inherits the requirements playbook’s discipline: a change states what and why, the traceability chain walks its impact across the five dimensions (scope, schedule, cost, risk, benefits), it is evaluated against the recorded decisions it touches, approved at the tier its consequence deserves, and the baseline is re-versioned. Under recorded baselines, most change stops being adversarial: a fired assumption is the system working, and the re-baseline that follows is a governed decision with its dignity intact. The change log is read at every steering as its own evidence stream, because the pattern of changes is the program’s honest biography.

Cutover and go-live readiness

Cutover is engineered, not celebrated. The runbook sequences every step with owners and timings; the rehearsal executes it end to end, including the rollback, because an unrehearsed rollback is a hope with a heading; reconciliation criteria for data are set at design time and met with evidence before the window opens; boundary health from the integration estate is on the dashboard before, during, and after; and the go or no-go is a gate in the full sense, decided on verification-tier evidence with the no-go path funded and undramatic. The morning after cutover, the technical event is over and the business event begins, which is the next section’s whole point. The deeper cutover runbook guide extends this discipline as it publishes.

Adoption and benefits

Delivery does not end at deployment, because deployment was never the point. The Adoption Ledger runs from before cutover to ninety days past it: target behaviors per role, evidence the systems already produce, owners in the business, reviews at 30, 60, and 90 days reported through the same evidence-backed cadence as everything else. Where behaviors are not landing, that is information about design, incentive, or workload, routed to an owner, not a communications failure to be messaged over. Benefits are then measured against the baseline’s own case, and the gap, in either direction, is owned and explained, because a benefits review that surprises everyone is a controls failure that waited nine months to be reported.

Recovery: when delivery slips

Slipping programs are recovered with the same instruments, applied harder. Re-establish truth first: an evidence pass across the critical path, because recovery planned on softened status recovers nothing. Re-baseline as a decision, with the fired assumptions named, rather than stretching the old plan until it snaps. Narrow the scope to what the benefits case actually needs, using the priority decisions already recorded. And re-earn the cadence: shorter, harder-evidenced, decision-per-meeting, until trend turns. Recovery is a governed re-decision, not a heroic phase; the full recovery diagnostic deepens this as it publishes.

Common mistakes

  • Baselines set in optimism with assumptions unstated, then defended as if they were facts.
  • Status softened one notch per week; the first red arriving after the last recovery option.
  • One methodology imposed on all workstreams because the organization “is Agile” or “does Waterfall.”
  • Gates that always pass; registers that are lists; standups that are status theater at higher frequency.
  • Change fought as combat until it arrives as crisis.
  • Cutover rehearsed never, rollback rehearsed less.
  • Demobilizing at go-live, exactly when the business event begins.
  • Recovery attempted on unre-established truth.

Delivery maturity model

LevelNameCharacteristics
Level 1HeroicDelivery by effort and escalation; status negotiated; every program starts from scratch
Level 2ControlledBaselines and registers exist; cadence runs; evidence appears where champions sit
Level 3EvidencedEvidence-backed status standard; change governed; gates decided on evidence, not attendance
Level 4AssuredProportionate assurance across the portfolio; cutovers rehearsed; adoption tracked past go-live
Level 5CompoundingDelivery runs as an enterprise capability: programs inherit decisions and controls, recoveries are rare and governed, and the benefits review holds no surprises

One habit per program, made standard.

Deliverables

  1. The baselined plan as recorded decisions: scope, schedule, cost, benefits, with triggers.
  2. The running control set: evidence-backed status packs, risk register, decision log, change log.
  3. The assurance record: gate outcomes and verification evidence at the recorded tiers.
  4. The cutover pack: runbook, rehearsal evidence, reconciliation results, go or no-go record.
  5. The Adoption Ledger with its 30, 60, and 90 day reviews.
  6. The closure record: benefits against baseline, fired assumptions, and the inheritance handed to the next program.

Learning path

This playbook is the delivery spine of the sequence, and its conceptual anchor is The Status Report That Tells the Truth, whose Evidence-Backed Status model runs its every control. Beneath everything, Requirements Are Decisions, Not Documents and its template govern the baselines and the change flow. The sequence it carries: Discovery, Requirements Management, Solution Design, Integration Strategy, Data Governance, and AI & Automation Strategy. Around it: Assurance Is What Lets Leaders Say Yes for the gates and tiers, and Adoption Is the Real Go-Live for the finish line delivery actually has. As they publish, the control cadence, recovery, and cutover guides deepen the craft beneath their sections here.

How this playbook uses the framework library

The pattern, at the point of delivery: Evidence-Backed Status runs the controls. The RDR Model governs baselines, decisions, and change. Proportionate Assurance tiers the gates and reviews. The Adoption Ledger defines the finish line. The Escalation Seam and boundary health guard the automated and integrated paths through cutover, and the Constraint Horizon’s rulings pace what the program can honestly promise. One system, one language, carried across the line: discovery, requirements, design, integration, data, automation, and the delivery that makes them real.
