About

I think in systems, lead through people, and measure in outcomes.

I’m an independent enterprise business consultant and project manager. For eleven years I’ve led ERP and operations programs, along with the cross-functional teams behind them, taking complex change from strategy through to a live, governed operation.

Organizations bring me in when the work has outgrown how it’s run. A stalled ERP rollout, systems that won’t talk to each other, rising cost with no obvious cause, or a program stuck between teams. I start with how the work actually flows and find the real constraint, which usually isn’t where people assume it is. Then I take it out of the process.

I’ve delivered across BPO, SaaS, ecommerce, logistics, and enterprise finance operations, on four continents and at real scale. The common thread is never the industry. It’s the operating problem underneath it.

Clients trust me because I translate between the boardroom and the operating floor, stay close enough to the work to clear blockers myself, and build the governance that keeps change in place after the project team leaves. I reach for technology and automation only when they remove real cost, never for their own sake.

Business first, technology second

I start with the outcome the business needs. Technology only earns its place when it removes real cost or risk.

Own outcomes, not activity

I hold myself and the team to results a business can feel, not a list of things that got done.

Consulting Process

Business first. Technology second. Results always.

A consistent, business-led method I bring to every engagement. Technology enters only once the problem and the process are clear.

01

Understand the business problem

Start with the outcome the business needs and the constraints in the way, not the org chart or the tooling.

I sit with the people who run the work and map goals, systems, risks, and the real constraint. Defining the problem correctly here is what keeps the rest of the work from going sideways.
02

Analyze the current process

Measure how the work performs today and where cost, delay, and risk concentrate.

I document the process end to end and baseline it: cycle time, errors, cost, and service levels. Opinions become numbers you can improve against and prove.
03

Design the future-state process

Shape how the work should run, the operating model, workflow, and governance, before any technology.

I design the future state for how teams actually work, so the change is adopted, not just correct on paper. Getting this right is what makes technology worth adding.
04

Select the right technology

Only now choose the systems and automation, and only where they earn their place.

With the process fixed, I choose the ERP, CRM, integration, or automation that removes real cost or risk, and deliberately leave technology out where a simpler change does the job.
05

Implement and manage change

Deliver to go-live and lead the adoption that makes it stick.

I lead delivery, coordinate teams, and manage risk, then drive the training, enablement, and governance that keep the new way of working in place after the project team leaves.
06

Measure business outcomes

Prove the result against the baseline and keep improving.

I measure outcomes against the baseline set in step two, tune what is not landing, and build the discipline that keeps the operation improving instead of drifting back.

Insights

Short perspectives on the questions leaders actually ask before, and during, a transformation.

Digital Transformation

Why digital transformations fail

Most fail for the same reason: they start with technology instead of the operating model. Buy tools before you fix how the work flows, and you automate the mess and call it progress. The ones that succeed define the business outcome first, then let that decide what technology, if any, is needed.

ERP

How to prepare for an ERP implementation

An ERP program is a business change, not an IT project, and the work that decides success happens before go-live. Clean data, agreed processes, honest scope, and executive sponsorship matter more than any configuration choice. Without them, no amount of implementation skill saves the rollout.

AI Strategy

AI isn’t the strategy, business value is

AI is a capability, not a plan. The question is never “where can we use AI,” it is “which problems are worth solving, and is AI the best way to solve them.” Start from the outcome and the ROI, and most of the hype falls away, leaving a short list where AI genuinely earns its place.

Operations

Signs your business has outgrown manual processes

When your team spends more time moving data between systems than using it, when errors climb with volume, and when leadership relies on stale spreadsheets to decide, the operating model has hit its ceiling. These are not people problems. They are signals the process needs redesigning before it costs you more.

CRM

Choosing the right CRM for growth

The best CRM is not the one with the most features, it is the one your team will actually use and that fits how you sell and serve. Start from your revenue process, not a vendor demo. A CRM that is adopted and clean beats a powerful one that becomes a data graveyard.

Process

How business process mapping saves millions

You cannot fix what you cannot see. Mapping a process end to end almost always reveals the real bottleneck, and it is rarely where people assume. Once the waste, handoffs, and rework are visible, the savings are usually obvious, and often large, before a single tool is bought.
